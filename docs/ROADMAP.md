## Next: real circuit geometry from OpenStreetMap

**This is the next major piece of work.**

The circuit outlines currently shipping are approximate shapes generated by
Claude Design. They are recognisably "a racing circuit" and nothing more —
Suzuka has no figure-eight, Mount Panorama has no mountain climb. Anyone who
knows these circuits spots it immediately, on the section the whole site is
built around.

The plan:

1. **Query.** For each circuit, fetch its way/relation from the Overpass API
   (`highway=raceway` and friends), by name or by a pinned OSM id per circuit —
   pinned ids are more reliable than name matching.
2. **Project.** Convert lat/lon to a planar coordinate space (Web Mercator is
   fine at circuit scale).
3. **Simplify.** Reduce to a sane point count with Ramer–Douglas–Peucker — enough
   to keep every corner's character, few enough to keep the `d` string
   reasonable.
4. **Normalise.** Scale and centre into the 1000 × 620 viewBox with a small
   margin, preserving aspect ratio.
5. **Emit.** One continuous closed subpath per circuit — a single `M`, absolute
   commands only, ending in `Z` — written into `data/tracks.ts` by the same kind
   of generator script already used for the current data.

The module itself needs no changes: the type, the animation loop and the
components only ever depended on "one continuous closed path in this viewBox".
The existing path-integrity tests become the acceptance check.

Open questions to settle while doing it:

- **Start/finish position.** Real ways begin wherever the OSM contributor
  started drawing. A `startFinishOffset` field (0–1 along the path) will
  probably be needed so the S/F marker and the lap origin land on the actual
  start line.
- **Figure-eight circuits.** Suzuka crosses over itself. A single subpath can
  express that as a self-intersecting loop, which is correct for
  `getPointAtLength()` — but the crossover needs checking visually, and the
  bridge/tunnel section may want a different stroke treatment.
- **Per-track display duration.** With real geometry the circuits differ a lot
  in length; a per-track `displayDurationMs` (rather than the global 12s) would
  let a long circuit read as long without becoming unwatchable.

## Also next

  replacing the handoff's placeholder numbers. Cheap, high-value, and it makes
  the section honest even before the geometry lands.

## Later

- **Image pipeline.** WebP/AVIF with `<picture>` fallbacks. `width`/`height`
  are done — every raster now declares its intrinsic size, which the hero
  marquee and the Sim to Real gallery both depend on for measurement. What is
  left is the encoding: 3.6 MB across 13 files, with `fiat-front.png` at 1.1 MB
  and `portrait-cutout.png` at another 1.1 MB, both photographs stored as PNG.
  Needs an encode step and therefore a dependency.
- **Lazy-load the track-records chunk.** It is the heaviest part of the bundle
  and sits below the fold.
- **Real sector splits.** The bars are currently even thirds of path length.
  Actual splits need sector distances per circuit — worth doing once real
  geometry exists, not before.
- **Legal pages.** `Gizlilik` and `Şartlar` link to `#`.
- **Social preview image.** `og:image` is deliberately absent — it wants a
  dedicated 1200x630 asset and none of the existing photography is that shape,
  so the card degrades to title + description. An `apple-touch-icon` PNG is
  also still missing; that one cannot be an SVG. The favicon itself is in place
  as a placeholder mark (`public/favicon.svg`), derived from the wordmark.
- **Mount Panorama's region.** Filed under `ASIA`; Australia is Oceania. Either
  correct the region or rename the tab — leaving Asia with a single circuit is
  the trade-off.
- **Component tests for the interactive parts.** Selection behaviour and
  playback intent are testable without a frame loop; that is exactly why
  `usePlayback` and `useLapAnimation` are separate hooks.
- **Analytics**, if it is ever wanted — privacy-preserving and cookieless, or
  not at all.

## Explicitly not planned

- **A CMS or an API.** Content is hand-maintained typed constants. A live
  social-stats fetch would need a server-side token, which a static site has
  nowhere to keep.
- **A light theme.** The site is dark-only by design; a theme provider for one
  theme is dead weight.
- **A contact form.** It would need a backend or a third-party endpoint. The
  mailto CTA is the design's answer.
- **Server-side rendering.** See [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md) for why
  Vite over Next.js.
